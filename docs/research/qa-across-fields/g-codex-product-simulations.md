**1. Maya, empirical economist**

On Monday, Maya opens the project for a paper on a training programme. Python imports the administrative records, Stata constructs the estimation sample, R draws figures, and a script builds the draft. Her coauthor has returned comments alongside a referee report. She tells Relay, “Start with the sample discrepancy; leave the specification alone.” A card represents an outcome she wants to hand to her coauthor, rather than every command required to produce it.

The first card is **“Explain the 143-person discrepancy between Table 1 and Table 3.”** The agent follows the existing scripts, compares participant identifiers, and produces a short attrition table. Done means every difference has an identified cause and both table notes describe their actual samples. Without another prompt, the agent can check duplicate identifiers, missing join keys and counts against the generated tables. It discovers that one filter excludes participants with missing follow-up dates. It must stop before deciding whether those participants belong in the analysis. Maya checks the pre-analysis plan and records the decision.

Tuesday’s card is **“Regenerate Table 3 using the agreed sample and standard errors.”** Done means the table comes from the committed scripts, uses the approved sample and inference settings, and agrees with the underlying estimates at its displayed precision. The agent can inspect those settings, run licensed Stata in batch, and compare the exported cells. A separate verification session repeats the relevant work and records exactly what it checked. Maya does not need to approve formatting repairs individually. She does need to approve any proposed change to the quantity the analysis estimates.

On Wednesday, the referee asks about participants who enrolled late. **“Answer referee comment 2 with the late-enrolment sensitivity analysis”** is a distinct request. Done means a reproducible comparison, a clear description of the changed sample, and a response paragraph that accurately describes the result. The agent finds the referee’s requested restriction in the supplied report and proposes the implementation. It can reconcile counts and coefficients automatically once the restriction is agreed. It cannot quietly describe this new analysis as preplanned, or choose the most favourable result for the response.

Thursday brings **“Update the abstract and results section to match the revised tables.”** Done means every changed numerical statement matches a named output, with units and uncertainty preserved, and Maya’s coauthor accepts the interpretation. The agent prepares the edited document and a compact list of changed sentences beside their supporting cells. Exact numerical reconciliation can be scripted; whether “the programme improved employment” overstates the evidence remains a coauthor question. The review opens the rendered draft in her existing viewer. Nobody asks her to inspect a terminal transcript before reading it.

Friday’s card is **“Prepare the replication package for the coauthor’s fresh-machine run.”** Done means the documented command reproduces the specified tables and figures on the coauthor’s machine, with prerequisites and inaccessible inputs identified. The agent can check file references, environment instructions and output comparisons. Missing restricted data produces “not reproduced,” never an invented successful substitute. The coauthor returns the run log through their existing collaboration channel; Maya attaches it to the card.

Her Switchboard is simply **Board**. The visible lanes are **To do, Working, Check results, Coauthor review, Done**; detailed planning stages stay inside cards. **Tests** becomes **Automated checks**, containing actual checks for sample construction, exported numbers and reproduction. Its history remains available, but she rarely needs reliability statistics. **Check** means **Check evidence** and retains its current non-running behaviour: it reports whether applicable evidence exists. Running happens through the listed command or “Run these.” **Profile** becomes **Performance**, tucked into the menu; it helps investigate a slow pipeline, not reproduce a paper.

Three interruptions expose what to remove. Monday, the full planning procession delays a straightforward investigation: hide intermediate stages and let the agent proceed within the agreed scope. Wednesday, an interactive coefficient inspection threatens to become another card: retain the existing small-work exemption. Friday, the coauthor’s successful run is absent from Maya’s local test history: accept an attached result tied to the relevant code, inputs and environment instead of demanding a local rerun.

A small proposed **Results** list would put Table 3, the sensitivity comparison and the draft within immediate reach, each linked to its producing run. Until then, card evidence links suffice. Maya keeps Relay because she can return after teaching all day and immediately see what changed in the paper, why it changed, and which decision still belongs to her.

**2. Jonah, policy analyst**

On Monday, Jonah receives a request for a Friday brief comparing three options for expanding a municipal service. The working materials are budget PDFs, a spreadsheet, previous briefs and interview notes. He uses a document editor and spreadsheet application throughout the week. Relay holds the working folder, delegated tasks and supporting files. It does not require his reviewer to install Relay or learn git.

His first card is **“Reconcile the service-cost totals in the budget and annual report.”** Done means a source table identifies the documents, editions, pages, periods and units, and explains the difference or leaves it explicitly unresolved. The agent reads the supplied files, extracts figures with page references, and checks their arithmetic. It can detect that one total includes capital expenditure while another excludes it. It cannot assume that explains the entire discrepancy without finding the relevant definitions. Jonah receives the two passages together, rather than a polished explanation unsupported by either document.

Tuesday’s card is **“Build the three-option cost workbook.”** Done means the workbook calculates the agreed options from identifiable inputs, recalculates in Jonah’s spreadsheet application, and separates assumptions from sourced figures. The agent can inspect formulas, identify broken references and compare totals with a separate calculation. Existing workbook conventions tell it which cells contain inputs. Where assumptions are missing, it asks once on the card. It must stop before choosing a politically convenient demand forecast or silently treating an uncertain estimate as an observed cost.

On Wednesday, **“Draft the two-page options brief with traceable sources”** produces the main deliverable. Done means the requested options are covered, factual statements have usable source references, and the rendered document fits the agreed length without hiding material limitations. The agent checks that references resolve, quotations occur in the cited material, and numbers match the workbook. It also flags passages that appear to overstate their sources. That last activity is editorial assistance, not a deterministic proof that the source supports the claim. Jonah reads the recommendation and its qualifications himself.

The reviewer then challenges an apparent saving. Thursday’s card is **“Explain why Option B’s first-year saving disappears by year three.”** Done means an understandable explanation linked to the relevant workbook cells, with the effect of the agreed demand range shown. The agent can vary those inputs and reproduce the comparison without repeated permission. It discovers that temporary funding masks recurring costs. Jonah decides whether the brief’s recommendation should change; the agent may propose language but cannot settle that tradeoff by maximising one spreadsheet cell.

Friday’s card is **“Resolve the reviewer’s comments and prepare the final brief.”** Done means every comment is answered or explicitly left open, the final document and workbook agree, and the reviewer accepts that version. The agent can compare comments against revisions, check for broken references, and rebuild the deliverables. It prepares the delivery files. Sending the brief to the client requires Jonah’s instruction; an accepted card does not itself authorise distribution.

Jonah calls the Switchboard **Board**, with **To do, Working, Check evidence, Reviewer review, Done** visible. Questions remain attached to their deliverable. **Tests** is labelled **Automated checks** and shows workbook reconciliation and document checks; prose without a runnable check is not disguised as a test. **Check** becomes **Check evidence**, showing the last applicable findings and gaps without secretly starting work. The AI verification lane prepares the evidence; the human lane asks, for example, “Does the revised recommendation fairly represent the recurring cost?” **Profile** disappears from the main row into **Performance** in the menu. He has no routine reason to open it.

Three moments make the current design cumbersome. Monday, filing a card for each extracted source would overwhelm the actual assignment: keep readings and extracts inside the deliverable’s working files. Tuesday, a workbook reader reports cached values while the real spreadsheet recalculates differently: remove any automatic pass based merely on successful file parsing, and retain “recalculation not checked” until the supported application runs it. Friday, copying reviewer comments into Human QA, a verdict and a resolution wastes time: keep one review record, linked from the card, and display its acceptance without re-entry.

The proposed **Results** list contains the current workbook, brief and source table, opening each in its normal application. It needs no built-in spreadsheet or document editor. Jonah keeps Relay because a reviewer can challenge a sentence and he can quickly reach the exact source passage or calculation behind it. The value is faster substantive revision, with fewer hours reconstructing where a number came from.

**3. Leila, computational scientist**

Leila’s lab produces fluorescence microscopy images. The instrument writes image files and acquisition metadata; a Python pipeline segments cells, calculates measurements and exports figures. Protocols and the lab notebook explain what happened at the bench. On Monday she opens Relay beside her existing image viewer. Her collaborator will rerun the analysis on another workstation on Friday. The week’s work concerns a particular experiment, not a general campaign to “improve pipeline quality.”

The first card is **“Import Monday’s plate images and reconcile the sample map.”** Done means every expected image is accounted for, files are linked to the recorded wells, and discrepancies are listed without altering the originals. The agent compares filenames, acquisition metadata and the supplied sample map. It can detect missing wells, duplicates and inconsistent dimensions without being prompted for each check. An image labelled differently in two records remains disputed. The agent must stop before deciding which label reflects what actually happened at the bench.

Tuesday’s card is **“Correct background subtraction for the unevenly illuminated images.”** Done means the agreed correction is implemented, reproducible before-and-after outputs are available, and Leila accepts its effect on representative images. The agent can test synthetic inputs, check output ranges and compare measurements across the supplied reference set. Those checks do not establish that real biological signal has been preserved. Leila opens the original and processed images in her viewer and looks at the difficult cases. The agent may propose a correction method; it cannot change the scientific measurement definition without her agreement.

Wednesday’s card is **“Rebuild Figure 2 from the approved measurements.”** Done means the figure regenerates from the identified inputs and code, plotted values match the measurement table, and its legend accurately describes the experimental units and exclusions. The agent checks sample counts, group labels, units and plotted coordinates. It catches a legend that calls individual cells independent experiments. It must ask Leila how the experiment should be summarised rather than deciding that more observations justify stronger evidence. Her collaborator reviews the scientific interpretation of the resulting figure.

Thursday brings **“Document the acquisition deviation for plate 17.”** Done means the notebook entry records the actual departure from the protocol, its supporting records and any unresolved consequences. The agent can assemble timestamps and settings from acquisition files, compare them with the protocol, and draft the entry. It cannot infer an undocumented incubation time from a normal-looking result. Leila supplies what she observed and approves the entry. Her words remain attributable to her; the agent does not retrospectively rewrite the experiment into compliance.

Friday’s card is **“Make Figure 2 reproducible on Arun’s workstation.”** Done means Arun follows the documented command from the agreed input files, obtains the expected measurements within the agreed tolerance, and can explain any remaining output differences. The agent prepares environment instructions, runs the package locally and compares the returned outputs. It checks input identities and software versions before interpreting numerical disagreement. It cannot call a rerun successful merely because an image file exists. Arun’s run and Leila’s figure approval answer separate questions, and both remain visible.

Leila calls the Switchboard **Board**, with **To do, Working, Reproduction check, Scientific review, Done** visible. Mechanical work can finish without a ritual human sign-off when the acceptance conditions were already settled. **Tests** becomes **Pipeline checks**, showing input validation, known-example checks and numerical comparisons, with execution history available for diagnosis. **Check**, labelled **Check evidence**, reports the existing evidence and its scope; it does not launch a costly analysis. **Profile** becomes **Performance**. Initially she uses a saved terminal command to profile this pipeline, since Relay’s existing app/build targets do not fit. The same table-first presentation could be reused later if demand warrants it.

Three interruptions determine what to remove. Tuesday, a required staged-app demonstration makes no sense: open the actual image comparison and drop the scenario machinery. Wednesday, several exploratory segmentation settings threaten to create a forest of cards: keep temporary runs as working files and record only the selected result and consequential decisions. Friday, identical-code reruns differ because the input export changed: remove any freshness verdict based on code alone and show which inputs were actually compared.

The proposed **Results** list holds Figure 2, its measurement table and the comparison images. It links to the notebook instead of becoming another notebook. Leila keeps Relay because a collaborator can reproduce a figure and understand its exceptions without an hour-long call. The pipeline work, experimental account and final figure stay connected while each remains in the tool suited to it.

**What Relay should build once**

The shared product is a short route from a request to the current deliverable, its supporting work and the remaining decision. Build a minimal output record: a file or bundle, its producing command where applicable, identified code and inputs, relevant environment information, and links to checks and review. Existing card links can carry the first version. Scripts or agents can supply the record; unsupported or unidentified dependencies should remain explicitly unknown.

Record outputs when someone intends to review, reuse or deliver them. Do not register every exploratory calculation. This preserves [the owner’s earlier project]’s strongest principle—the researcher must be able to understand the contractor’s work—without importing its entire research model into Relay. Direct terminal work must remain available. People will still inspect a variable, recalculate a cell or open an image without first describing an assignment.

The common acceptance record should distinguish passed checks, failures, missing evidence and unresolved judgment. Acceptance applies to an identified version. A source or input change can invalidate that applicability; an unchanged file cannot establish correctness. Required checks come from the agreed outcome, not the accidental presence of a Tests section. Runs should update their displayed status without requiring another Check click.

Resolve the policy contradictions before expanding the interface: expectations precede implementation; a separate verification session records what it actually checked; agents may move cards within their authority; human judgment remains pending where required. Support Human QA without duplicate prose. Keep intermediate stages available but visually quiet, and preserve the small-work exemption.

The genuinely different parts belong in project instructions, scripts or optional integrations: economic specification conventions and pre-analysis commitments; source extraction and spreadsheet recalculation; instrument formats, calibration rules and experimental protocols. Their viewers also differ. Relay should open the right artifact and retain the review, without building a universal statistical IDE, office suite or lab notebook.

**Results is a separate thing from the Test suites pane.** A figure or brief remains useful even when it has no tests. A test remains useful even when it produces no deliverable. Results should initially be a compact list of selected outputs with links to their runs, checks and reviews. Automated checks should retain their own execution history and diagnostics.

Do not add a fifth permanent toolbar button to achieve this. Put Results beside Board when outputs exist, and tuck infrequent tools into the menu. The product test is whether these people retrieve and revise their work faster with fewer interruptions—not whether Relay can collect a more elaborate account of everything they did.
